# Layout, files, and automated processing

## Typography and density

MIT recommends 10–12 pt text, readable fonts such as Arial, Calibri, or Times New Roman, and 0.5–1 inch margins [M]. UK National Careers Service recommends those font examples at 11 pt or larger [N]. These are audience-specific recommendations, not universal hiring or ATS thresholds. Use the more readable overlap as a starting point.

The following are Resume Pro design defaults, not researched hiring standards:

| Element | Working default | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | 11–12 pt | Compare apparent size at actual print scale; fonts differ. |
| Section headings | 12–14 pt, bold | Clear hierarchy without oversized headings. |
| Name | 18–24 pt | Preserve room for relevant evidence. |
| Margins | 0.65–0.85 in (about 16.5–21.5 mm) | Adjust within readable bounds and submission rules. |
| Line spacing | 1.05–1.15 | Inspect wrapping, accents, and text collisions after export. |
| Paragraph/bullet spacing | 2–4 pt after | Use consistent rhythm rather than empty lines everywhere. |
| Section spacing | 8–12 pt before, 3–6 pt after heading | Avoid headings stranded at page bottoms. |
| Page size | Letter for a US target, A4 for Brazil/UK as a starting assumption | Follow explicit instructions and confirm other markets. |

Use an available readable font; do not promise that Arial, Calibri, or Times New Roman is installed or embeddable in every environment. Check glyphs such as ã, ç, é and the candidate's name. Inspect substitution/embedding and export fidelity where tools allow. No evidence here establishes that any font improves hiring outcomes.

Use left-aligned body text, consistent indents, restrained bold, and high contrast. Keep dates consistently formatted and clearly attached to roles; avoid ambiguous numeric dates across markets. Edit duplication and weak content before shrinking type or margins. Do not compress a readable two-page resume into a cramped single page.

Page length is a relevance/career-stage/market/instruction decision. MIT's one-page preference and exceptions are student-oriented [M]; Brazilian PUC-Rio guidance permits more length for experience [B2]. Neither supplies a universal rule. As a practical starting point, try one page for sparse early-career evidence, two when relevant history warrants it, and more only with a clear audience need. Academic/specialized formats require separate guidance. Keep related employer/title/date lines together, avoid orphaned headings, and inspect every page, including the last.

## Five distinct stages

| Stage | What it means | What resume work can improve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parsing | Extracting text and populating candidate fields | Reading order, complete labels, selectable text. |
| Recruiter searches/filters | Finding or narrowing candidates by terms/fields | Accurate explicit qualifications, recognizable terminology. Verify the actual platform for claimed syntax or behavior. |
| AI matching/scoring/ranking | Model-assisted comparison to configured criteria | Evidence and clear wording; algorithms/settings vary. |
| Automatic disqualification | Employer rules tied to application responses or other configured criteria | Truthful answers; wording cannot supply a genuinely missing eligibility requirement. |
| Human assessment | People evaluate evidence and make decisions | Relevant accomplishments, clear scope, readable presentation. |

Greenhouse Talent Matching uses employer calibration and AI match scores; its documentation says it does not itself advance or reject candidates [G2]. Greenhouse Auto-reject is a separate employer-configured application-answer rule [G3]. Do not generalize either feature to every ATS, infer enabled settings, or treat any rejection speed as proof of a mechanism. Do not set arbitrary “ATS score” targets, invent hiring statistics/reading times, or promise a pass.

## Compatibility choices

A simple single column with recognizable section headings is a practical compatibility default, not a ban on visual design. Greenhouse documents parsing difficulties with columns, graphics/photos/word art, images, tables, headers/footers, text boxes, spaced-out letters, and unclear sections [G1]. Tables are not proven to fail everywhere, nor is every template incompatible. Keep contact details and employment history in the normal document body and logical reading order, not only in decorative/sidebar/header elements.

Follow the employer's requested format and upload limits. Neither PDF nor DOCX is always better. A PDF can preserve appearance yet have broken extraction; a DOCX can reflow or substitute fonts. Image-only scans lack a normal text layer. OCR may help but requires checking names, dates, accents, and order; do not claim OCR was performed when unavailable.

Greenhouse documents a 2.5 MB parsing limit and describes failed parsing as an attachment requiring manual field entry, not automatic rejection [G1]. Recheck this limit before giving current platform-specific advice. Do not alter truthful employer names/titles just to satisfy parser heuristics.

## Artifact verification and delivery

Use an available document or PDF skill when generating/editing those files. Within its workflow:

1. Compare final claims, dates, ownership, qualifications, and links against supplied evidence.
2. Render and inspect each page at readable scale: clipping, crowding, broken glyphs, awkward breaks, hierarchy, and contact visibility.
3. Extract text locally where tools permit. Read it in sequence, checking missing/duplicated text, merged columns, associated employer/title/dates, accents, and contact information. Selectable text alone is not enough.
4. If extraction conflicts with appearance, simplify structure, re-export, then repeat both checks. Preserve a visual version if requested and deliver a clearly identified compatibility version as needed.
5. Report tools/checks actually used and any remaining limitation. Successful local extraction is useful evidence, not a test of every ATS. Say “actual ATS tested” only when that test occurred with authorization; do not upload to external parsers/scorers without permission.

Keep unresolved essential issues visible in accompanying advice. Do not label an artifact submission-ready merely because it looks attractive.
