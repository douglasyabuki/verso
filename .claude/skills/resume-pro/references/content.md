# Content, evidence, and tailoring

## Choose the evidence before the section sequence

Use role, level, country, language, instructions, and supplied history as decision inputs. Maintain a compact working ledger: fact/source; ownership; scope; result; uncertainty. The ledger is an internal aid, not a mandatory extra deliverable. Resolve conflicting dates or titles rather than choosing the more impressive version. Compare each rewritten claim literally: do not narrow “reset steps” to “password-reset steps” unless confirmed, or infer a tool, audience, method, or outcome merely because it is typical.

Harvard [H] and MIT [M] recommend ordering sections by importance and recent entries first. These are professional recommendations, not software requirements. Practical adaptations:

| Candidate | Likely opening evidence after contact details | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student/first job | Education and relevant projects, research, volunteering, or placement | Let projects lead if they demonstrate the target work better; never manufacture paid experience. |
| Experienced professional | Relevant employment achievements | Education moves down unless a recent qualification is central to the transition or eligibility. |
| Career changer | Short evidence-backed bridge summary, relevant projects/skills, then employment | Make transferable work explicit without relabeling prior employment as the new profession. Retain dated history. |
| Senior leader | Brief leadership proposition and recent leadership outcomes/scope | Prioritize decisions, organizational scope, people, governance, and business evidence where actually supplied. Student page limits do not govern executives. |

A required license or certification can be prominent; technical skills can lead for a technical role, but support them with examples. Leadership can lead when it is the strongest relevant evidence, whether paid or voluntary. Do not duplicate evidence just to fill sections.

A summary earns space when it clarifies a transition, specialization, or senior scope in a few specific lines. Omit a generic summary that merely repeats job titles or says “results-driven.” A targeted role label may suffice. This is a practical editorial default, not a mandatory section.

Section relevance and entry chronology are separate decisions. Within work history, use reverse chronology, accurate dates, and clear employer/title associations. If splitting relevant and additional experience, retain dates and make the grouping explicit. Within a role, order bullets by relevance and strength, not by the order supplied. Allocate more space to the evidence that matters; compress older work without creating misleading chronology.

## Write from supported facts

Convey action, context/scope, and result where available. A result can be a completed deliverable, a resolved issue, an adopted process, or a qualitative outcome supported by the candidate. Some useful bullets simply explain essential work. Do not force a numerical impact or identical formula onto every contribution.

Ask focused questions when useful: What did you personally do? Who used the work? What changed? Is the number documented, an estimate, or unknown? Preserve units, timeframes, baselines, currency, and uncertainty; do not turn a rough estimate into a precise causal claim. If no metric exists, write useful qualitative evidence and move on.

Replace clichés, vague duties, unsupported superlatives, and inflated verbs with concrete objects and actions. Repeated accurate verbs are preferable to inaccurate synonyms. Retain necessary industry terms; expand unfamiliar important acronyms when useful. Use past tense for finished work, present tense for ongoing responsibilities, and past tense for completed achievements in a current job. Keep punctuation consistent. [H, M]

These examples are fictional; every detail in each “after” is supplied in its note:

- Before: “Responsible for reports.” Note: compiled weekly stock reports and flagged discrepancies for purchasing. After: “Compiled weekly stock reports and flagged discrepancies for the purchasing team.” No efficiency result is implied.
- Before: “Amazing team player who transformed onboarding.” Note: wrote setup instructions used by new hires; did not own onboarding. After: “Wrote setup instructions used by new hires during onboarding.”
- Before: “Helped build an app.” Note: course group built a booking app; candidate implemented form validation, no real users. After: “Implemented booking-form validation for a team course project.” Do not claim a product launch.
- Antes: “Profissional proativo com inglês excelente.” Nota: encaminhava solicitações de clientes por e-mail em inglês; não informou nível. Depois: “Encaminhamento de solicitações de clientes por e-mail em inglês.” Do not infer fluency.

## Tailoring without changing eligibility

Use a compact map, explicit or internal:

| Posting criterion | Required/preferred/unclear | Supplied evidence | Match/transferable/gap/unknown | Resume action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example: SQL reporting | Required | Candidate built SQL sales reports | Match | Move that bullet up; name SQL accurately. |
| Example: regulated license | Required | Candidate explicitly lacks it | Gap | State gap in advice; do not imply eligibility. |

Distinguish absence of evidence from confirmed lack. Prioritize required criteria, then high-value preferences; lack of a preference is not automatic ineligibility. Do not assume a stated requirement can be waived. Suggest clarification from the employer or a better-fit role where helpful, while still producing useful truthful content.

Use posting terminology only where it accurately names existing work; an adjacent skill is not the requested skill. Demonstrate important skills in experience/projects rather than relying solely on a list. Without a posting, target the stated role and flag assumptions about specialization or seniority. Never generate an invented match score.

## Sensitive or incomplete histories

- Gaps: retain truthful dates. Offer a short neutral explanation only when useful and authorized; no need to disclose health, family, or other private details. Do not stretch employment to conceal gaps. [N]
- Overlap: label concurrent, contract, part-time, or volunteer work only when confirmed. Do not assume an error.
- Unfinished degrees: state in progress with a confirmed expected date, or coursework/study with actual dates and no completed degree implication. Never invent equivalency.
- Limited experience: projects, unpaid work, research, and caring/community responsibilities can provide relevant evidence if the candidate wants them included; label them accurately.
- Outdated/irrelevant experience: compress or omit when it does not help the application; keep enough dated history to avoid a deceptive account. A selected resume is not a requirement to list every activity.
- Coursework/certifications: retain relevant differentiators, required credentials, accurate issuer/status/expiry where material; remove excessive introductory coursework or obsolete credentials that imply current competence.
- Soft skills/proficiency bars: replace unsupported labels and decorative scales with examples or accurate plain-language proficiency. Keep useful technical terminology.
