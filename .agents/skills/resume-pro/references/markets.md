# Markets, language, and conditional content

## Default focus

Use en-US and US employment-resume conventions unless the user specifies otherwise. Switch to pt-BR resume writing or Brazilian-market guidance when requested; do not infer that request from source language, names, or nationality. The Brazilian and UK guidance below is conditional reference material, not a checklist for default US resumes. An explicit target country overrides the market default without automatically changing the requested application language.

## Resolve the application type

“CV” can mean an employment resume in the UK or Brazil and an academic career record elsewhere. Ask what the receiving institution expects, not merely what the document is called. For academic applications consult the institution/professional body; in Brazil check whether Lattes is requested [B3]. For public-sector forms, grants, clinical posts, or creative portfolios, research the actual instructions. Do not apply employment page limits to them.

Keep country, application language, and candidate nationality separate. A Brazilian candidate explicitly applying in English to a UK employer needs UK-targeted advice. When no destination is specified, use the US default; ask about destination only when conflicting context materially changes the result.

## Conditional content decisions

These are practical defaults; specific application instructions and current local sources can change them:

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Contact | Name, usable phone/email, location relevant to the work arrangement, and useful professional links. Avoid unnecessary full street address or identifiers by default. |
| Photo | Omit by default for ordinary employment resumes in the US/UK/Brazil unless the application context calls for it. Harvard discourages it; Brazilian MG says only if requested [H, B1]. Other markets or performing/modeling applications need specific guidance. |
| Personal details | Do not add age, marital status, national IDs, or nationality by habit. A legitimate eligibility question belongs in the requested application channel; accurately state work authorization only when supplied and relevant. No legal inference from a name, address, or language. |
| Objective/summary | Omit generic aspirations. A targeted objective can clarify a first job/transition when expected; a summary must contribute concrete evidence. Neither is universally required. |
| References | Usually omit names/contact details and boilerplate unless requested. UK guidance allows a reference statement while Harvard omits references [N, H]; follow application expectations and protect referees' details. |
| Salary | Keep expectations/history out of ordinary resume content unless specifically requested in this context. Do not invent an amount. Brazilian PUC-Rio discourages salary details [B2]. |
| Links | Include a relevant, accessible portfolio/GitHub/LinkedIn with a recognizable label; do not rely on icons alone. Inspect content/permissions when authorized; do not publish or create accounts. Remove broken or irrelevant links. |
| Interests | Include only when they supply useful evidence or fit a specific audience; they should not crowd out stronger qualifications. |

## English and Brazilian Portuguese adaptation

Translate meaning and professional register, not the prestige of a title. Preserve names, employers, dates, facts, team attribution, metrics/units/currency, and qualification status. Keep the official credential/title where useful and add a plain explanation instead of inventing degree equivalence or upgrading “assistente” to “manager.” Flag ambiguous titles for confirmation.

Use natural phrasing in the requested language and its specified regional variant, including accompanying advice. Keep unresolved proficiency notes outside candidate prose: “Inglês: uso em e-mails” can describe supplied evidence without appending “nível não informado.” For unfinished study, natural pt-BR wording such as “Administração — curso interrompido em 2020 (não concluído)” preserves the absence of a degree. In Portuguese, either concise action verbs or parallel action nouns can work; choose a consistent pattern that fits the candidate's voice. Do not mechanically translate every English verb. Preserve accurate product/tool names and distinguish self-reported language ability from a tested CEFR or other level. No invented certification.

For dates, an English month-year format and a Portuguese month-year format can reduce ambiguity. Adapt decimal separators only when meaning stays fixed; do not silently convert currency or recalculate achievements. Confirm actual paper size and exported accents. Make a fact-by-fact comparison between versions; a more fluent translation must not add leadership, seniority, or outcomes.

## Source disagreements and limits

- **Personal details:** MG's current internship guidance lists birth date/age/address, and undated PUC-Rio guidance lists marital status [B1, B2]. UK guidance excludes age/birth date/marital status/nationality [N]. Resume Pro chooses minimal relevant contact data by default, as a privacy/editorial judgment rather than a claim that all Brazilian employers or laws require it. Verify requested fields; do not blindly import either list.
- **Soft skills:** MG permits personality qualities; PUC-Rio favors technical over behavioral skill lists [B1, B2]. Prefer concrete evidence over unsupported labels, regardless of country.
- **Length:** Brazilian student guidance itself differs: MG favors up to two pages, PUC-Rio allows two to three with experience [B1, B2]. Use audience and relevant evidence, not a national page-count stereotype.
- **Templates and acronyms:** MIT broadly cautions against templates, while Harvard offers templates; Greenhouse identifies specific structural problems [M, H, G1]. Inspect the actual file rather than banning templates. Harvard broadly discourages abbreviations; keep necessary industry terms and expand important acronyms when useful instead of erasing accurate terminology.
- **AI authorship:** Harvard favors AI as an editing aid [H]. This skill also supports user-requested drafting from evidence, with candidate voice and factual review; it does not represent that broader drafting workflow as Harvard's recommendation.

For another market or consequential specialized advice, consult current public employment, university, institutional, or professional-body guidance. Check audience, update date, and whether the advice addresses the application at hand. Add direct sources and limits to accompanying advice; do not present a government domain as proof of universal correctness.
